# Research method

Use this method for candidate collection, verification, and ranking. The research target is public English- and German-language fitness content from Europe, the United States, and Canada, not a fixed list of creators.

## Category definitions

### Trend

An emerging topic, behavior, claim, product, audio use, training method, or conversation that appears in at least three relevant public posts from at least two independent creators during the research window. A single exceptional post is an outlier, not a trend.

### Format or challenge

A repeatable video structure, test, rule set, or fitness challenge visible in at least two independent public posts. A single post may qualify only when it is a strong verified outlier and the report labels it single-origin candidate rather than an established format.

### Viral hook

The first spoken or on-screen promise used by a 30-second-plus video with strong outlier evidence. Record a short verbatim hook only when a transcript or frames verify it; otherwise paraphrase the mechanism and set exact wording to unknown. Keep any quotation short.

## Candidate search

Search English and German sources from Europe, the United States, and Canada in parallel across YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram Reels. Use creator location, profile context, language, event location, or another public regional signal when available. Do not collect candidates from other regions unless the user explicitly expands the scope. Expand into public trend pages, hashtag or keyword results, and fitness media only to discover direct posts. The final evidence must link to the original public post whenever accessible.

For a full scan, inspect at least 60 and at most 150 candidates. Continue until every category has 15 verified findings or the accessible retrieval budget is exhausted. If the 7-day window is too sparse, extend once to 14 days and disclose the extension.

Do not use favorite creators as a required starting set. Creator diversity is evidence against overfitting to one account.

## Viral and outlier evidence

Prefer relative performance over raw views:

1. Compare the post with the median views of up to 10 recent, accessible, same-platform videos from that creator.
2. Use videos of comparable age when possible.
3. Compute outlier_ratio = post_views / creator_recent_median_views.
4. Treat outlier_ratio >= 2.0 as strong public outlier evidence.
5. If a baseline is unavailable, use public views-per-day, engagement signals, and cross-creator recurrence, but cap confidence at medium.

Never claim that a post is viral when neither a baseline nor meaningful public performance evidence is available.

## Evidence record

Capture for every included item:

- stable research ID
- category
- platform and creator
- direct public URL
- published date or unknown
- retrieval timestamp
- duration or best supported estimate
- visible views, likes, comments, and shares when available
- creator baseline and outlier ratio when calculable
- exact short hook or hook mechanism
- transcript source: native, auto-caption, local transcription, frames, or unavailable
- recurrence evidence for trends and formats
- observed facts
- interpretation
- confidence: high, medium, or low

## Confidence

- high: direct source, verified date and duration, usable metrics, and either a creator baseline or multiple independent corroborating posts.
- medium: direct source exists, but baseline, transcript, or one important metric is incomplete.
- low: discovery is relevant but evidence is partial. Include only when needed to explain a coverage gap, not as a top finding.

## Quality checks

- Every report item has its direct source beside it.
- Every 30-second-plus claim is supported or explicitly marked as estimated.
- Facts and interpretation are visibly separate.
- Repeated uploads and near-duplicates are deduplicated.
- English-language discoveries are not down-ranked merely because the final report is German.
- No original idea, rewritten hook, script, production advice, own-video comparison, or university angle appears in the findings.
