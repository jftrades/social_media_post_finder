# Creator format inspiration for YouTube

Use this reference for format mechanics, not creator imitation. The observations below are title- and channel-format patterns visible on the creators' public YouTube video pages on 2026-09-15. They do not justify claims about private strategy, retention data, or exact scripting inside videos.

Sources:

- [Will Tennyson videos](https://www.youtube.com/@WillTennyson/videos)
- [Riley Rehl videos](https://www.youtube.com/@rileyrehl/videos)

## Divide the jobs

- **Noah-inspired logic:** make one open question govern the story; every beat changes the probability of the outcome.
- **Will-inspired packaging:** make a broad fitness premise instantly legible through contrast, scale, access, or escalation.
- **Riley-inspired experience:** give the experiment a human baseline, a finite commitment, diary-like progress, and an honest personal change.
- **Ferdi's layer:** anchor the idea in his real hybrid-training conflict, student constraints, food behavior, humor, physical sensations, and available footage.

Never borrow a creator's exact premise, title, joke, personal story, or exaggerated superlative. Combine mechanisms only when they reveal something real about Ferdi.

## Will Tennyson: premise and packaging engines

His visible catalog repeatedly makes the whole video understandable before the click. Useful engines include:

1. **Investigate an extreme environment:** enter a place, system, subculture, or claim that already contains tension.
2. **Test every option in a bounded category:** compare several meaningfully different methods and finish with a verdict.
3. **Two-endpoint contrast:** cheap versus expensive, healthiest versus unhealthiest, beginner versus elite, promise versus reality.
4. **Levels or escalation:** each chapter is harder, stranger, more expensive, or more specialized than the last.
5. **Identity or routine swap:** eat, train, or live under another person's rules, with a clear comparison to Ferdi's baseline.
6. **Timed immersion:** a real 24-hour, multi-day, or one-week constraint changes normal life and creates repeated tests.
7. **Finish-line confinement:** remain in a location or continue the experiment until a measurable total is reached.
8. **Public test or outside judgment:** other people attempt, rate, predict, or expose the gap between expectation and reality.

What to adapt at Ferdi's scale:

- Replace “world's most extreme” with a locally provable comparison.
- Replace expensive access with a student-budget rule, Munich location, TUM context, or hybrid-athlete constraint.
- Use three genuinely different chapters; three similar exercises are not escalation.
- Put the clearest contrast in the title and thumbnail, then save the verdict for the video.

## Riley Rehl: challenge plus personal progression

Her visible catalog repeatedly packages self-improvement as a lived experiment: 30 days of calisthenics, 60 days of running, one week of 30,000 daily steps, 100 miles of cycling, a week of triathlon training, or an Ironman series. Useful engines include:

1. **Beginner-to-test arc:** establish the honest starting point, train for a finite period, then repeat the same test.
2. **Diary with checkpoints:** each chapter records a new obstacle, adaptation, or small proof of change.
3. **Attempt-first honesty:** “I tried” leaves room for stopping, failing, revising the plan, or discovering the wrong goal.
4. **Series with a destination:** individual episodes answer a local question while moving toward one larger event.
5. **Life friction:** travel, work, dating, sleep, food, or confidence changes how the fitness plan feels and whether it survives.
6. **Specific but relatable reset:** an ordinary problem becomes clickable through a finite rule and visible before/after state.

What to adapt for Ferdi:

- State his true baseline instead of pretending to be a beginner.
- Use the half-marathon as the destination, but give every episode its own question and payoff.
- Preserve ugly first attempts, doubts, shortened sessions, and plan changes; they are evidence, not filler.
- Let performance data support the emotional story rather than replace it.

## Strong format combinations for Ferdi

Use these as generators, not finished ideas:

- **Local contrast + verdict:** test three Munich/TUM/student versions of the same fitness problem and rank them on one visible score.
- **Beginner skill + retest:** choose a skill Ferdi cannot already do well, record a baseline, learn under a time limit, and repeat the identical test.
- **Hybrid conflict experiment:** change one variable and measure whether running performance improves without destroying a named strength marker.
- **Budget versus performance:** compare the cheapest realistic fueling plan with Ferdi's usual approach using cost, stomach feel, energy, and result.
- **Escalating levels:** complete beginner, intermediate, and advanced versions with a consequence or unlock between stages.
- **Expert prediction versus reality:** get a coach or experienced athlete to predict Ferdi's result, then test it visibly.
- **One-day investigation:** visit several accessible places or people to answer one concrete fitness question; do not disguise a normal vlog as an investigation.
- **Rule-based day:** one unusual but safe rule affects training, eating, commute, or recovery and produces repeated decisions.
- **Race-prep stress test:** simulate one specific race condition—hills, fueling, pacing, or fatigue—without pretending it reproduces the whole race.
- **Viewer-controlled fork:** let a poll, wheel, or opponent determine meaningful choices while one score remains constant.

## The click-and-story gate

A long-form concept is strong only if all six answers are clear:

1. **Topic:** Can a stranger understand what happens in one sentence?
2. **Gap:** Is there a result, winner, verdict, transformation, or breaking point they cannot know yet?
3. **Proof:** Can the outcome be shown through a timer, distance, repetitions, money, ranking, test, or visible before/after?
4. **Escalation:** Can the middle contain at least three changes in difficulty, knowledge, location, rules, or prediction?
5. **Personal reason:** Why is Ferdi the right person to attempt this now?
6. **Production truth:** Can he film it honestly within his time, access, budget, and safe training load?

If the answer to **Gap**, **Proof**, or **Escalation** is no, repair the premise before generating titles.

## Safety and honesty guardrails

- Never add extreme mileage, sleep deprivation, binge eating, dehydration, punishment workouts, dangerous supplementation, or training through injury only to make a premise clickable.
- A hard but safe test may be intense; define a stop condition and keep it compatible with the current training week.
- Do not frame running as payment for food or food as a moral failure.
- Do not promise a body transformation that the duration cannot honestly produce.
- Do not use “every,” “perfect,” “impossible,” “world's,” or a numerical claim unless the video can substantiate it.
