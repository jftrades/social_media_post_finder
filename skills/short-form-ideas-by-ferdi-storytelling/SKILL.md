# Short Form Ideas by Ferdi Storytelling

Create stories that make Ferdi relatable and motivating through honest effort, setbacks, and renewed action. His achievements are evidence inside the story, never the reason viewers are told to admire him.

## Load the right context

- Before drafting, read [references/ferdi-story-bank.md](references/ferdi-story-bank.md). Use only details that pass its relevance and sensitivity rules.
- Read [references/noah-method.md](references/noah-method.md) for the evidence-derived storytelling mechanics. Abstract the method; never reuse a creator's wording or personal story.
- Read [references/ferdi-style-calibration.md](references/ferdi-style-calibration.md) before writing dialogue or voice-over. Treat it as a voice and specificity benchmark, not a reusable template.
- For a planned shoot, Day in the Life, What I Eat, food ranking, Over/Under, training week, Road-to, challenge, or deep seated story, also read [references/format-playbook.md](references/format-playbook.md).
- For YouTube concepts, measurable challenges, creator-inspired packaging, title/thumbnail promises, or requests mentioning Will Tennyson or Riley Rehl, also read [references/creator-format-inspiration.md](references/creator-format-inspiration.md). Do not load it for an ordinary short-form recap unless the request needs a larger format engine.

## Understand the request

Ferdi will often provide unstructured spoken notes. Extract silently:

- the real event or planned test;
- the measurable goal, question, or contradiction;
- what Ferdi expected beforehand;
- what actually made the outcome uncertain;
- the concrete actions and available footage;
- the result, if known;
- the honest feeling and why the event mattered;
- the useful or relatable point for the viewer.

### Intake for a general storytelling request

If Ferdi only says that he needs help with storytelling, wants to make a story, or otherwise has not supplied enough context to draft, ask these four short questions together before writing:

1. **Worum geht es genau?** What happened or is planned, what is the central event/question, and how long should the video be?
2. **Persönlicher Bezug?** Does Ferdi want a specific experience or part of his story included, or should the video stay entirely in the present?
3. **Welches Footage gibt es?** Distinguish among existing B-roll only, a seated/direct-to-camera recording, a future location where the camera will be taken along, or a mixture. Ask which concrete clips already exist or can realistically be filmed.
4. **Welche Emotion?** Positive/uplifting, deep and vulnerable, light/funny, tense/challenge-driven, or a stated mixture?

Ask only for items that are genuinely missing. If Ferdi's initial notes already answer some or all four, do not repeat those questions. When enough information is present, draft immediately.

Footage is a structural input, not an afterthought. Build the story around what can be shown:

- With **B-roll only**, write a clean voice-over whose claims are visibly supported by the available clips.
- With **direct-to-camera footage**, allow pauses, facial reaction, and more personal sentences; avoid constant visual narration.
- For a **planned on-location shoot**, give a compact capture plan and short live-reaction prompts before drafting the final outcome.
- For a **mixed format**, identify which emotional lines belong on camera and which action beats belong in voice-over.

After the intake, ask at most two additional short follow-up questions only when a missing fact would materially change the story, especially an unknown result, target, duration, or filming constraint. For a future event with no result yet, create branches for plausible outcomes rather than inventing one.

For a YouTube challenge request, also establish the real baseline, measurable end condition, available time, locations/people/equipment, safe stop condition, and the one image that could prove the premise in a thumbnail. Do not ask again when Ferdi already supplied them.

## Build the story

Prefer a controlled information gap: reveal the topic, goal, and stakes early while withholding the outcome or explanation. A vague mystery hook is not enough.

### Preserve specificity

Before drafting, identify the concrete anchors already present in Ferdi's notes: exact place, moment, number, impulsive action, physical state, literal thought, consequence, and next plan. Keep the strongest anchors in the script. Do not compress a vivid scene into a generic phrase such as “ich habe mich für eine neue Challenge entschieden.”

- Prefer “Ich saß in der Bahn, sah, dass der Lauf genau in meinen zwei USA-Wochen stattfindet, und kaufte impulsiv den Startplatz” over “Die Anmeldung war spontan.”
- Prefer the actual thought “Jeder längere Lauf fühlt sich an, als würde ich meine Gym-Progress wegwerfen” over “Ich hatte einen inneren Konflikt.”
- Prefer “unter zwei Stunden”, “viermal Gym”, or the three named run types over “ich möchte gut vorbereitet sein.”
- Keep messy but revealing details when they explain the decision. Polish grammar and pacing without sanitizing Ferdi's personality.
- Use at least two concrete anchors in an ordinary story and more when Ferdi supplies them. Do not invent specifics to satisfy this rule.

Use this flexible sequence when it fits:

1. **Hook:** concrete event, number, test, contrast, or emotionally specific problem.
2. **Setup:** minimum context required to understand why this is difficult.
3. **Friction:** real doubt, mistake, fatigue, conflicting goal, or unexpected condition.
4. **Progress:** actions and intermediate evidence that change the viewer's prediction.
5. **Re-hook:** a new problem, surprising intermediate result, or sharpened question before attention drops.
6. **Payoff:** show or state the result only when the original gap has earned resolution.
7. **Meaning:** one personal realization or useful takeaway tied to the event.
8. **Next loop:** a natural question, choice, or next challenge when one genuinely exists.

Not every video is a challenge. A result-first recap may reveal the result immediately and withhold *how* it happened. Food and routine formats may use accumulation, a running score, a budget, or an unusual rule as their engine. Deep stories may use before → pressure → choice → imperfect change → present-day meaning.

## Keep it honest instead of flexy

- Lead with a viewer-relevant tension, not a résumé of TUM, work hours, sport frequency, weight loss, or past follower count.
- Use an achievement only if it changes the stakes or proves the transformation being discussed.
- Pair an impressive fact with the honest uncertainty, cost, mistake, or unfinished part—but never manufacture weakness for optics.
- Make the viewer think “das kenne ich” before “krass, was er alles macht.”
- Do not imply that busy viewers lack discipline. Show that Ferdi also loses time, doubts the plan, gets disappointed, and starts again.
- Do not force university into fitness, food, or humor videos. TUM is context only when study pressure changes the event.
- Do not force school history, bullying, alcohol, body image, or old social-media success into ordinary formats.
- Avoid toxic positivity. The message is not that every problem disappears through effort; it is that a small next action can restore agency.
- Prefer concrete behavior and scenes over claims such as “I am disciplined,” “I work harder,” or “never give up.”

## Voice

Write natural spoken German in Ferdi's rhythm: direct, young, self-aware, sometimes dry or lightly chaotic. Controlled phrases such as “Digga”, “komplett bodenlos”, “ausgelaugt wie Sau”, “hauptsache” or “ich krieg's gerade nicht ganz geschissen” may fit when they sound earned. Do not stack slang or imitate a generic motivational influencer.

Keep sentences speakable. Preserve useful imperfections and self-corrections when they add personality, but remove rambling and repeated setup. Avoid corporate coaching language, exaggerated tragedy, fake cinematic lines, and generic slogans.

Use Noah-inspired sentence logic without copying his wording: event first, then “aber/deswegen/heißt”, then the next concrete consequence. Favor one idea per spoken sentence and clear cause-and-effect over polished abstractions. Internal thoughts should sound like thoughts Ferdi would actually have, not like a narrator diagnosing his own character.

Avoid replacing Ferdi's details with phrases such as “ich möchte herausfinden, was möglich ist”, “das Ganze ist widersprüchlich”, “ich stelle mich einer neuen Challenge”, or “meine Reise beginnt”. Such lines are usable only after the script has established exactly what happened and why it matters.

## Required output

Choose the matching contract. Do not force a YouTube ideation request into two short-form scripts.

### Short-form story request

Always return exactly two genuinely different variants:

### Variante 1 — Ereignis/Challenge

More concrete and propulsive. Usually begins with the event, number, rule, or test and delays the result.

### Variante 2 — Ehrlich/persönlich

More reflective and contradiction-led. It may foreground the doubt, flawed expectation, or emotional meaning while still containing visible action.

For each variant provide:

1. **Angle** in one line.
2. **Text-Hook** suitable for the first 5–10 seconds.
3. **Voice-over or spoken script** with `[RE-HOOK]`, `[PAYOFF]`, and `[CTA]` marked inline.
4. **Compact filming/B-roll notes** only for scenes Ferdi has or can realistically capture.
5. **Anti-flex check** in one sentence: why the viewer is invited into the story rather than asked to admire Ferdi.

Match the requested duration. If none is given, target roughly 40–60 seconds and say so. Keep the CTA natural: a real choice, shared experience, next experiment, or useful viewer question. Do not add “Folgt für mehr” by default.

### YouTube format or challenge request

Return the requested number of concepts. If none is given, return 10. For every concept provide:

1. **Working title:** clear topic plus one honest contrast, number, rule, or uncertainty.
2. **One-sentence premise:** what Ferdi actually does and why the outcome is not obvious.
3. **Measurement:** a visible success criterion, comparison, score, or before/after test.
4. **Escalation:** three distinct chapters that update the viewer's prediction rather than repeat the same task.
5. **Payoff:** the result or decision the opening promise earns.
6. **Production reality:** why it is filmable with Ferdi's stated time, body, budget, access, and current training plan.

Rank ideas by click clarity, story depth, and feasibility when Ferdi asks which ones are strongest. Reject or repair concepts that are merely a normal workout with a dramatic title, depend on fake extremes, have no visible measurement, or require reckless fatigue. When he asks for a full script, choose the concept first and then build its cold open, chapters, re-hooks, payoff, and capture plan.

## Final checks

Before answering, verify:

- The topic is clear within the opening sentence.
- The information gap is specific and not misleading.
- Every personal detail is relevant to this event.
- At least one real turn or re-hook changes the expected outcome.
- The payoff answers the original question.
- The emotional point follows from what happened.
- Both variants differ structurally, not just in wording.
- No claim, result, medical implication, or personal history was invented.
- For YouTube concepts, the title promise, measurement, escalation, and thumbnail proof all describe the same actual video.
