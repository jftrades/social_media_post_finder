# Format playbook

Read only the section that matches the request. All formats still receive exactly two variants under the main skill contract.

## 1. Real event after it happened

Use when Ferdi describes a run, gym session, swim, exam-day workout, failure, PR, meal, or unexpected moment.

Extract:

- Before: expectation or assumption.
- Test: what Ferdi actually attempted.
- Friction: what made the outcome uncertain.
- Evidence: footage, numbers, reactions, intermediate attempts.
- Result: factual outcome.
- After: what he did, felt, or changed.

Build the story around the largest contrast between expectation and reality. Do not start with the entire day if the decisive test happens later.

## 2. Planned event or challenge

Use when the outcome is not yet known.

Deliver:

- filmable premise and success criterion;
- expected difficulties;
- essential shots before, during, and after;
- short live questions Ferdi can answer on camera;
- outcome branches for success, near miss, failure, and unexpected result;
- a closing question that remains valid regardless of outcome.

Never script a fake success or breakdown. Plan containers for honest reactions.

## 3. Day in the Life

Avoid a flat chronological diary. Give the day one governing question, such as:

- Can this schedule work without every session destroying the next?
- Will Ferdi train after losing an hour to scrolling?
- Can he fuel work and training within a student budget?
- Which planned task will fail first?

Use time and recurring visual checkpoints. Let at least one plan change. TUM or work belongs only when it materially constrains the day.

## 4. What I Eat in a Day

Choose one engine rather than merely listing meals:

- total cost or cost per meal;
- protein per euro;
- fueling before and after a specific session;
- enjoyment versus optimization;
- unusual repeated food with a running counter;
- whether the plan survives a long work/study day.

Do not moralize food or call every choice “clean.” Use taste, price, practicality, fullness, and performance as distinct evaluation axes.

## 5. Food ranking or student-athlete test

Define the score before filming. Possible dimensions:

- price;
- protein per euro;
- calories or carbohydrates per euro when relevant;
- taste;
- portability;
- usefulness before or after training.

Create a visible score and save one surprising result for late in the video. Do not force a personal trauma callback; the premise already contains a gap.

## 6. Over/Under or weird-habit challenge

Use an odd but plausible behavior and a measurable threshold:

> Over or under X by the end of a real day?

The item, number, rule, and reason must be original and true to Ferdi. Use placeholders if unknown. Build running totals and checkpoints. A small complication—availability, fullness, price, schedule, training—should change the prediction.

Do not reproduce another creator's exact mandarin premise. Variations should change at least the item, rule, context, and payoff.

## 7. Training week or hybrid update

Avoid “four Gym sessions and two runs” as the whole hook. Find the week's real conflict:

- legs versus second run;
- muscle progress versus longer runs;
- knee tolerance;
- adding swimming without ruining recovery;
- work/study schedule versus one key session;
- whether a change solved last week's failure.

Track one question across sessions. Each day should update that question rather than merely name the workout.

## 8. Road-to series

Define:

- measurable destination;
- honest current baseline;
- time horizon if real;
- today's smallest test;
- one variable being changed;
- visible scoreboard;
- unresolved next step.

Do not imply linear progress. Plateaus, missed sessions, conflicting goals, and revised plans are legitimate episodes.

Current candidate: first San Francisco half marathon while maintaining strength and muscle. Confirm race date, current baseline, knee status, and actual target before writing specifics.

## 9. Deep seated story

Choose one present-day question and only the history that explains it.

Useful arcs:

- poor school performance → fear/shame → realizing the work was for himself → choosing a difficult degree → still failing an early exam → continuing imperfectly;
- early social success → public embarrassment at school → stopping something he liked → trying to prove a different identity → returning to creating now;
- appearance insecurity → gym as attempted correction → rigid optimization → appreciating function and health without pretending all insecurity disappeared;
- teenage abstinence and extreme rules → missed enjoyment and unmet goals → learning that consistency and pleasure can coexist.

Avoid a victory montage. End in the unresolved present: Ferdi still doubts, scrolls, struggles, or experiments, but now returns to action sooner.

## 10. Doomscrolling story

Do not make the message “delete your phone and become disciplined.” Show the second-order trap: scrolling causes anger, and anger wastes even more of the day.

Potential engine:

1. Concrete lost-time admission.
2. What Ferdi had planned.
3. Anger and temptation to write off the day.
4. Small action such as leaving for the gym, walking, swimming, or preparing food.
5. Honest outcome: better, unchanged, or only partially recovered.
6. Viewer question about restarting after a bad block of time.

## Filming prompts

When planning footage, prefer evidence:

- clock or elapsed time;
- attempt and intermediate result;
- imperfect face/reaction immediately afterward;
- relevant environment: desk, commute, locker, pool, track, supermarket;
- one object carrying the premise: goggles, food receipt, race entry, loaded bar, running vest;
- final scoreboard or message sent to someone.

Do not request reenacted crying, fake scrolling, or a staged failure. If a past event lacks footage, use present-day reflection, neutral archive material Ferdi owns, or simple text rather than fabricated scenes.
